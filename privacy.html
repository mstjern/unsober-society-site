Privacy Policy

Version 2026-11-20. In effect from 20 November 2026.

Unsober Society is an iPhone app made and run by Magnus Stjernström, an individual. He is responsible for the data described here. “We” and “us” mean him and anyone he asks to help moderate the app.

In short: what you post is seen only by the members of the groups you post it to. The app has no ads, no analytics and no tracking, and no data is sold or shared for advertising. Your exact position is never sent to our server. You can delete your account in the app at any time.

What we store, and who can see it

  • Your sign-in. You sign in with Apple; there is no other way to sign in. We store the account identifier Apple gives the app. The app asks Apple for your name, and uses your first name only to suggest a display name. If Apple passes an email address (often a private relay address), it is stored with your sign-in and not used for anything else. Nobody else in the app sees this.
  • Your profile: your display name, your profile photo if you add one, and when your account was created. Your name and photo are seen by people who share a group with you, and by the owner of a group while your request to join it is open.
  • Your groups: the groups you create or join, their names, who is in each one and since when, who owns it, its invite code, and your requests to join (with whether they were approved or declined). Members of a group see its name and members. A group’s owner sees requests to join it.
  • Your check-ins: the photo, an optional caption, the venue name and city, when you posted, and which groups you chose. A check-in is seen only by people who were members of one of those groups when you posted it. Someone who joins later never sees earlier check-ins, and someone who leaves or is removed stops seeing them.
  • A map point on a check-in, unless you turn off “Show on map” when posting (see Location).
  • Your reactions (🍻 or 🖕) and when you gave them. They are seen with the check-in by people who share a group with you.
  • Device tokens, only if you allow notifications, so Apple can deliver them to your phone. Nobody sees them. When you sign out, the app asks our server to remove your phone’s token.
  • A record of each notification: what kind it was, who it was for, who caused it, the check-in or group it is about, when, and whether it was delivered. Phones are recorded only as a one-way code (a hash), not the device token. Nobody sees this.
  • Reports you make (what you reported, the reason, an optional note, and when) and the people you block. Reports are seen only by us. Your blocks are seen only by you; the person you block is not told.
  • Which version of the Terms you accepted.

Photos

Check-in photos are taken with the camera inside the app or picked from your library. Before a photo or profile photo is uploaded, the app shrinks it and removes all its metadata, so no location or camera details leave your phone. Photos are kept in private storage. The app opens them through short-lived links that our server gives only to people allowed to see them. A notification about a check-in carries a link to its photo that works for one hour, so your phone can show the photo with the notification.

What we do not collect

  • No analytics, no advertising and no tracking. The app contains no third-party analytics, advertising or tracking code.
  • No contacts, and no access to your photo library beyond the photos you pick, one at a time, for a check-in or your profile.
  • Your exact position never reaches our server, and we keep no history of where you have been.
  • No data is sold, and none is shared for advertising.

Kept only on your phone

Your sign-in session, your answer to the drinking-age question, and which groups you last posted to (so the app can pick them again).

Location

Location is optional and used only while the app is open, never in the background: when you check in, and to show where you are on the map.

  • Suggesting the venue. Your phone asks Apple Maps for bars and restaurants within about 150 m of you. Your phone also asks Apple for the name of the neighbourhood and city you are in. Your exact position is not sent to our server.
  • The map point. With “Show on map” on (the default), a check-in carries one point. If you picked a suggested venue, it is the venue’s own location. Otherwise it is the centre of the roughly 500 m square you were in, with the neighbourhood’s name; your phone rounds it before sending, and our server rounds it again. With “Show on map” off, or without location, a check-in carries no point.
  • 12 hours. The map shows only check-ins from the last 12 hours, to members who can see them anyway. After 12 hours nobody can see a check-in’s map point, and it is deleted within about an hour after that, whether it was a venue’s location or a 500 m square with its neighbourhood name.
  • The venue name and city stay part of the check-in for as long as it exists. When a check-in has a city but no point, a viewer’s phone asks Apple Maps where that city is, to place it on the map.
  • Your own position on the map (the blue dot) is drawn by your phone and never sent anywhere.

Notifications

If you allow them, the app sends notifications through Apple’s push service when someone in your group checks in, when someone reacts to your check-in, when someone asks to join a group you own, and when your own request is approved. A check-in notification shows the person’s display name, the venue and city (or the caption), and the photo. Nothing is sent between people who have blocked each other. We are notified of new reports. You can turn notifications off at any time in iOS Settings, under Unsober, Notifications.

Services that process data for us

  • Supabase: the database, sign-in, photo storage and the function that sends notifications. The data is stored in the United States (Supabase’s us-east-2 region, Ohio).
  • Apple: Sign in with Apple, push notifications, and Apple Maps for venue suggestions and place names.
  • GitHub: hosts these pages. These pages use no cookies and no analytics.

These services may keep short-lived technical logs, such as IP addresses, as part of running their service. We do not use them to track anyone.

Magnus, as the operator, can see everything that is stored, in order to run and fix the app.

How long data is kept

  • Your data is kept while you have an account.
  • You can delete any of your check-ins at any time; the app then deletes its photo too. Deleting a check-in also deletes its reactions and the notification records about it.
  • You can change or remove your profile photo; the app deletes the old file.
  • A check-in’s map point (the venue’s location or the 500 m square) can no longer be seen after 12 hours, and is deleted within about an hour after that.
  • Leaving a group, or being removed from one, ends your access to its check-ins straight away. Your earlier check-ins stay visible to the group’s other members who could already see them, until you delete them.
  • A check-in you report is hidden from you straight away. A check-in reported by two different people is hidden from everyone until we have reviewed it.
  • A check-in we remove for breaking the terms is hidden from everyone, including the person who posted it.

Deleting your account

Delete your account in the app: tap your profile photo (or initial) at the top of the feed, then tap Delete account. The app tells you what happens, and Apple asks you to confirm by signing in once more. If you cancel that, nothing is deleted.

Deleting your account removes:

  • your profile, display name and profile photo;
  • your sign-in, and the app’s link to your Apple ID, which is disconnected at Apple as Apple requires;
  • all your check-ins and their photos, captions, venues and map points;
  • your reactions, your group memberships and your requests to join;
  • your device tokens and the notifications sent to you;
  • the people you blocked, and the reports you made.

A group you own passes to the member who has been in it longest. A group with no other members is deleted.

If you cannot use the app, write to us and we will delete your account for you.

Children

The app is not for anyone under the legal drinking age where they live. If you believe such a person has an account, write to us and we will delete it.

Your requests

You can see and change most of your data in the app, and delete all of it there. To ask for a copy of your data, a correction, or anything else about your data, write to us.

Changes to this policy

If this policy changes in a way you need to agree to, the app asks you to accept the new version before you go on.

Contact

For questions, reports about content, or requests about your data, write to magnus@stjernstrom.com.
